# 07. Psychology and the classic mistakes

Markets are a machine for turning human reflexes into losses. Every reflex
below is natural; the plan exists to override them.

## The mistakes, in the order beginners make them

1. **Trading before learning.** Opening the account, buying a tip, losing 15%
   in week one. Fix: 30-day paper period, chapter 08.
2. **Oversizing.** "It's a sure thing" so 40% of capital goes in. Fix: the
   formula. Size is never a decision.
3. **No stop, or a mental stop.** The stock falls through the level and you
   "wait for it to recover". A 2% loss becomes 30%. Fix: GTT stop on fill.
4. **Averaging down.** Buying more of a loser to lower the average cost. You
   now have your biggest position in your worst idea. Fix: never add below entry.
5. **Cutting winners, holding losers** (disposition effect). Taking +0.5R
   profit "before it disappears" and letting -1R run to -3R. Fix: half at 2R,
   trail the rest; stops are not negotiable.
6. **Revenge trading.** Re-entering immediately after a loss to "make it
   back". Fix: daily loss limit of 2R, close the laptop.
7. **Chasing.** Buying a stock after a 10% up day because it is on the news.
   Fix: entries only at defined setups; the scanner's `from_20d_high` near 0
   with RSI above 75 is a chase, not a breakout.
8. **Switching systems every month.** Nothing is given enough trades to be
   judged. Fix: 50 trades per setup before changing anything.
9. **Trading the P&L, not the chart.** Exiting because the rupee number
   scares you. Fix: hide the P&L column; think in R.
10. **Options for "limited risk".** Buying weekly NIFTY options because the
    premium is "only Rs 3,000". Time decay and implied volatility crush make
    this the fastest way to lose 100% of many small positions. 9 in 10 lose.
    Fix: no derivatives in year one.
11. **Tips and groups.** If the tip came from someone with 2 lakh followers,
    you are the exit liquidity. SEBI fines these people regularly. Fix: none
    needed if you only trade your own written setups.
12. **Overtrading in chop.** The market is range-bound, breakouts fail, you
    keep trying. Fix: regime filter; cash is a position.

## Cognitive biases, briefly

- **Loss aversion**: a loss hurts about twice as much as an equal gain feels
  good. This is why stops feel terrible and why you must automate them.
- **Recency**: the last three trades feel like the truth. They are noise.
- **Confirmation**: once in a trade you see only bullish news. Decide the exit
  before entry, when you are neutral.
- **Overconfidence**: after a winning streak, size creeps up. Fix: the formula
  does not know about your streak.
- **Gambler's fallacy**: "it's fallen five days, it must bounce". Trends
  persist more than they reverse at daily horizons; the model's `ret_5`
  feature exists to let the data say so.
- **Sunk cost**: "I've already lost 20%, I'll wait to break even." The market
  does not know your entry price.

## Signs you should stop for the week

- You checked the price more than five times during work hours.
- You placed an order you had not planned the evening before.
- You widened a stop.
- You are thinking about how much you need to make back.
- You are angry at a stock.

## What a healthy relationship with trading looks like

- Most days there is nothing to do, and that is fine.
- A -1R loss is a business expense, journaled in two minutes and forgotten.
- A +3R win is noted without excitement; the next trade is sized identically.
- The plan changes once a quarter at most, from journal evidence, in writing.
- Your SIP in an index fund continues regardless and probably outperforms
  your trading account for the first two years. That is normal.
